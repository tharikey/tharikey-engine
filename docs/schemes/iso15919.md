# ISO 15919

`iso15919` · **text** · reversible (lossy) · **scholarly.**

ISO 15919 is the international Indic-transliteration standard (IAST's modern successor) — single letters
with diacritics: long vowels as macrons, retroflexes as dots-below. The **useful direction is
Thaana → ISO 15919** (an academic romanisation), though the forward map works too.

Source: the ISO 15919 column of
[*Romanization of Dhivehi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Dhivehi) (Romero-Frías).

| `k` ކ | `v` ވ | `m` މ | `f` ފ | `d` ދ | `t` ތ |

Note ISO's conventions: `ṣ` is the native retroflex sibilant ށ, dental `d`/`t` are ދ/ތ, and the
retroflexes `ḍ ṭ` are ޑ/ޓ.

## Glottal

The glottal stop is `ʾ` (U+02BE) ↔ އް — a scholarly choice (it isn't in the published table).

!!! note "Known gaps"
    The Arabic-loanword (*thiki*) consonants `ޘ–ޢ` are deliberately omitted: their scholarly ALA-LC
    forms would collide with ISO 15919's native `ṣ` (ށ) / `ṭ` (ޓ), breaking reversibility. They are
    religious / Arabic-text territory — the same gap as Malé Latin. This (plus the gemination↔glottal
    overlap) is why the scheme is `lossy`.
